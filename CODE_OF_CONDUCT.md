# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be kind. Assume the other person is trying to get something right. Argue about the code and
what LangGraph does, not about the person.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics,
gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Standards

Things that help:

- Welcoming and inclusive language.
- Respecting differing viewpoints and experiences, including differing views on what counts as
  a breaking change.
- Accepting constructive criticism gracefully. If someone finds a change graphlock misses, they
  have done the project a favour, and that is true no matter how it was phrased.
- Focusing on what is best for the people whose deploys rely on this.

Things that do not:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.
- Condescension about what someone does not know yet. LangGraph's internals are large and
  undocumented; "you should already know this" is never a useful contribution.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying standards and will take fair corrective action in
response to behaviour they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive or harmful. They may
remove, edit or reject contributions that do not align with this Code of Conduct, and will
explain moderation decisions when asked.

Report unacceptable behaviour by opening a private security advisory on the repository, which
is the only private channel this project has. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated
promptly and fairly, and the reporter's privacy and security will be respected.
